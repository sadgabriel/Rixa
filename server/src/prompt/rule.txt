[RULE — CORE GAME RULES]

This document defines the immutable rules and structural principles
of the game. These rules describe what the game IS,
not what a specific task SHOULD DO.

This game assumes and permits unrealistic, exaggerated,
and absurd elements as a normal state of the competitive context.
Unrealism is not an error condition.

────────────────────────────────
1. GAME STRUCTURE AND PHASE SEPARATION
────────────────────────────────

1.1 The game consists of distinct conceptual phases, including:
    - context setup,
    - competition analyze,
    - competition narrate

1.2 Each phase serves a different purpose and must not be conflated.
1.3 Results produced in any phase, once confirmed,
    become part of the persistent game state.

────────────────────────────────
2. CANONICAL STATE AND INTERPRETATION
────────────────────────────────

2.1 Context and faction definitions, once finalized,
    constitute the canonical state of the game.
2.2 Canonical state must be respected by all subsequent resolutions.
2.3 During an explicit context expansion phase,
    canonical information may be extended or reinterpreted,
    but must not be directly contradicted.
2.4 Canonical state represents established conditions and facts;
    interpretations, implications, and meanings may evolve over time.

────────────────────────────────
3. UNREALISM AS A BASELINE ASSUMPTION
────────────────────────────────

3.1 The competitive context may violate real-world physics, logic,
    scale, causality, or common sense.
3.2 Such violations are intentional and must not be treated
    as inconsistencies to be corrected or normalized.
3.3 Unrealistic properties of the context or factions
    are part of canonical state once finalized.
3.4 Absurdity, exaggeration, and excess define the possibility space
    of competition and must not be suppressed
    by realism-based reasoning.

────────────────────────────────
4. FACTIONS AND RESOURCES
────────────────────────────────

4.1 Each faction is associated with exactly three resource types.
4.2 Resources represent abstract strategic capacity,
    not literal physical quantities.
4.3 A resource value of zero does NOT imply total narrative absence.
4.4 Resources may be material, emotional, ideological,
    symbolic, or otherwise non-physical in nature.
4.5 Resources exist to express pressure, constraint,
    and long-term consequences rather than immediate capability.

────────────────────────────────
5. COMPETITION AS QUALITATIVE RESOLUTION
────────────────────────────────

5.1 Competitions are resolved through qualitative judgment,
    not numerical simulation.
5.2 Outcomes are determined by narrative plausibility,
    contextual effectiveness, and relative risk,
    not by the absolute power implied by setting descriptions.
5.3 Extreme or reality-breaking capabilities
    do not guarantee deterministic success.

────────────────────────────────
6. ASSERTION AND RESISTANCE PRINCIPLES
────────────────────────────────

6.1 Assertive or offensive actions do NOT consume resources.
6.2 Resources may be affected only as a consequence of
    failed or insufficient resistance.
6.3 Resource loss reflects strain, disruption,
    or accumulated cost rather than instant destruction.

────────────────────────────────
7. RESOURCE SCARCITY AND CONSEQUENCES
────────────────────────────────

7.1 Resource scarcity increases vulnerability and pressure,
    but does not create absolute impossibility.
7.2 Even when numeric resources are low or depleted,
    factions may still act with narrative consequences.
7.3 Scarcity should influence future outcomes
    through escalating risk rather than hard denial.

────────────────────────────────
8. COMPETITION CONSEQUENCES AND CONTEXT IMPACT
────────────────────────────────

8.1 Competition outcomes may have implications
    beyond immediate success or failure.
8.2 Such implications may affect:
    - faction perception, morale, or identity,
    - contextual tension, instability, or tone,
    - or the direction of future competitive narratives.
8.3 These implications represent interpretive impact,
    not automatic canonical change.
8.4 Canonical changes may only occur
    during an explicit context expansion phase.

────────────────────────────────
9. CONTINUITY AND IRREVERSIBILITY
────────────────────────────────

9.1 Once a competition outcome and its consequences are confirmed,
    they become irreversible parts of the game state.
9.2 Subsequent resolutions must respect accumulated outcomes.
9.3 Retroactive alteration of resolved events is not allowed.

────────────────────────────────
10. NARRATIVE FREEDOM AND CONTROLLED CHAOS
────────────────────────────────

10.1 Narrative surprises, twists, and extreme developments
     are an intended feature of the game.
10.2 Such developments must not contradict
     established canonical conditions or confirmed outcomes.
10.3 Chaos enriches the game only when
     internal consistency is preserved.
